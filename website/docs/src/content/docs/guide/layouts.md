title: Layouts
---

The menu at the top left of the window switches layouts:

- **Player** works like a music player and mixes for you. One bar along the
  bottom of the window shows the track you hear: its album art, title,
  artist, BPM and key (colored by its place on the key wheel, so keys that
  mix have neighboring colors), and what Automix plays next. In the middle
  are **Previous**, **Play/Pause** and **Next** over a thin line through
  the track: white for what has played, and a dim lilac where the next mix
  happens. **Next** cuts straight to the next track in Up Next, as other
  players do (the **Mix now** button in the Automix panel mixes into it
  instead). Point at the line and it turns lilac and shows the time there;
  click or drag to move through the track. The time played and the track's
  length are either side. On the right are the master volume, the mini
  player button, the karaoke (microphone) button and the queue button, which shows or hides **Up Next**
  (the Automix list), and under them the file's quality: format, bit depth
  and sample rate in lilac for lossless files (**FLAC 16-BIT 44.1 KHZ**),
  or the bitrate in grey (**MP3 320 KBPS**). The **heart** beside the title
  puts the track playing in **Favorites** (a playlist made at the top of
  Playlists the first time), or takes it out; it is filled lilac while the
  track is there. Right-click it to add the track to a crate or playlist,
  or to a new crate.
  Click the album art or the microphone on the bar for **karaoke**: the
  art large over the whole window, with the title, artist, album and year
  under it, and the lyrics beside it as on the karaoke screen (see
  [Lyrics](lyrics.md#in-player-mode)). The side panel hides while it
  shows. The art or the microphone again, the ⌄ at the top left, or the
  queue button goes back to the library.
  The **mini player** (**⇧⌘M**, **View › Mini Player**, or its button on
  the bar) is a small square window of album art that stays on top of the
  others. Point at it and the art dims and shows the title, artist, a
  progress line (click it to move through the track), **Previous**,
  **Play/Pause** and **Next**; **✕** brings back the main window, which
  hides while the mini player shows. It has no title bar: drag the art to
  move it, and the grip in its bottom right corner to resize it (it stays
  square). The **moon** on the bar is a sleep timer: after 15, 30 or 45
  minutes or an hour the music fades out over ten seconds and stops, or
  pick **End of track** to stop when the track playing now ends (Automix
  switches off so nothing mixes in). Point at the moon to see the time
  left; **Turn off timer** in its menu cancels it. **Order** and **Repeat** are at the
  top of Up Next. With Repeat on, a played track goes back to the end of Up
  Next instead of leaving it, so the list keeps going round.
  Up Next shows the track playing on top, under **Now playing**. Each
  mix follows the panel's **Length** and **Style**, unless you pick one
  for a single track: right-click it in Up Next and choose under **Mix
  into it** (**Panel** goes back to the panel's). A row with its own mix
  shows it in lilac, such as **⤵ 32 bars · Cut**.
  Click the artist on the bar, an artist in the search box's results, or
  **Go to Artist** in a track's menu, and their **page** opens over the
  list: their tracks in your collection, most played first, and their
  albums, newest first. Click an album (or **Go to Album**) for its page,
  with its tracks in order. **Play** plays the page now, **Shuffle** in a
  random order, and **Mix** in the order that mixes best by key and
  tempo; clicking a track plays it now (on an album, with the rest after
  it). The arrow at the top left goes back; picking a list closes the page.
  Player opens on **Home**: what you played and added lately, your
  crates and playlists, and mixes made from your collection by genre and
  decade (each a page of up to 50 tracks in the order that mixes best).
  Crates and mixes show four of their tracks' covers with their name on
  a bar. The
  house button beside the search box comes back to it.
  **Keep going**, under them, is Player's cruise control, off at first:
  while it is on, Up Next never runs dry. When fewer than two tracks wait,
  a track that mixes with the last one goes on the list (close on the
  Camelot wheel, close in tempo, not played in the last 3 hours, not the
  same artist twice in a row), and the next three picks show greyed at the
  end of Up Next, so you can drag in something else first. **From** picks
  where they come from: **Everything**, or the list the browser shows.
  Switching it on also switches Automix on. Right-click a track and pick
  **Start Radio** (the track plays next, then picks from everything) or
  **Start Artist Radio** (the artist's tracks and those sharing their
  genres), or right-click a crate or playlist and pick **Start Radio** to
  keep going from it.
  The keyboard's **play/pause**, **next** and **previous** keys, the
  buttons on headphones, and the system's Now Playing (Control Center on
  macOS, the media overlay on Windows, the desktop's media controls on
  Linux) work Player as its buttons do, and show the track with its art.
  The DJ layouts leave them alone unless **Media keys in every layout** is
  on in **Settings › Player**, so a headphone button can't stop a deck
  in the middle of a set.
  The top bar keeps to search. The search box (**⌘F**) is Player's only
  search box: it filters the track list and shows the best matches as you
  type: artists and albums first, then tracks (tracks first when a title is
  the better match). Point at one, or move with **↑** and **↓**, and pick
  **Play next**, **Add to queue** (the end of Up Next) or **Play now**,
  which mixes into it straight away; on an album these take the whole
  album in order, and on an artist **Show tracks** searches for them. The keys are **↵**, **⇧↵** and **⌘↵**; **Esc**
  clears the box and the list's search. There is no MASTER button (the
  bar's volume is the master volume); **Rec** shows only while recording
  (start it from **File › Record Mix**), and there is no **Mic** button
  (switch to another layout to use the microphone).
  Underneath, decks 1 and 2 take turns and [Automix](automix.md) mixes from
  one to the other. **Play** switches Automix on, **Next** mixes into the next
  track straight away, **Previous** goes back to the start of the track, and
  **Order** puts Up Next in order (shuffle, BPM, harmonic and so on). The
  large waveforms and the FX bar are hidden in Player.
- **2-Deck** shows the full decks, with jogs, tempo faders, pads and loops,
  and the mixer between them (see [Decks and mixer](decks.md)).
- **2-Deck Basic** shows two lighter decks without jogs or tempo faders, the
  slim mixer between them and the pads and loops in a bar under them
  (**PADS** shows or hides it, **JOG** adds a jog to each deck). It is off
  at first: switch it on with the layout menu's pencil.
- **4-Deck** shows four decks side by side, in the order 3, 1, 2, 4, as djay Pro
  does. Each deck is a strip with its own EQ, filter, gain and channel fader,
  PLAY and CUE, loop, pads and effects. A bar under the strips holds each
  deck's crossfader side, **SYNC** and tempo, either side of the crossfader;
  click a deck's tempo there for key lock, quantize and a tempo reset. With
  **WAVEFORMS** lit in the top bar, the four large waveforms are stacked above
  the decks in the same order.
  A [Groove Box](groove-box.md) deck shows its step grid only in 2-Deck.
- **Prep** shows deck 1 alone, for getting tracks ready (see [Prep](#prep)).

The button at the bottom left of the window hides the decks for a compact
view in 2-Deck and 4-Deck.

## Your layouts

The layout menu offers only **your layouts**. At first that's Player,
2-Deck, 4-Deck and Prep, in that order. To change them, click the
**pencil** at the top right of the layout menu. It turns the menu into a
list of every layout, each with a switch, as the side panel tabs' and the
library rail's choosers have:

- Switch a layout on to put it in the menu, or off to take it out. The
  layout you're on, and the last one on, can't be switched off.
- Drag a row by its grip (the six dots at its left) to move it up or down the menu.
- **Reset to default** goes back to Player, 2-Deck, 4-Deck and Prep.

Click the lit pencil to go back to the menu.

A controller's VIEW button (on the DDJ-FLX4, **SHIFT** + right **LOAD**)
goes to the next of your layouts, and from the last back to the first.
Layouts added to openDJ in later versions start switched off.

In openDJ 0.6 **2-Deck** was called 2-Deck Pro, and **2-Deck Basic** was
called 2-Deck. Your layouts carry over.

## Appearance

**Settings › Appearance** picks **Dark**, **Light** or **Match
system**, and so does the row at the foot of the layout menu (**Auto**
there is Match system). Dark is the default. Light is easier to read in daylight and at
outdoor gigs: panels, the library, buttons and Settings turn light, while
the big waveforms and the overviews stay on black with their colours, as
they read best there. Lilac text turns a deeper lilac so it stays readable
on white, and the Player layout drops its striped rows. **Match system**
follows your computer's light or dark setting, also when it changes while
openDJ is open. The mini player and the karaoke screen stay dark.

## Prep

Prep shows deck 1 alone, with no mixer, crossfader or effects, and opens the
browser on **In Prep** (see
[Stages](music.md#stages-inbox-in-prep-and-play-ready)). The In Prep list has
its own columns, to see what each track still needs: **Grid** (✓ locked,
✓ edited, ? check when the analysis wasn't sure, the number of tempo
sections, or detected), **Cues** (how many hot cues), **Tags** (✓ with a
genre and at least one of your tags), **Cover**, **Ready** (checklist lines
ticked) and **Score**. Pick other columns from the header's menu as
in any list; In Prep keeps its own set. Load a track and work through it:

- The large waveform shares the zoom of every other layout (see
  [Waveform zoom](decks.md#waveform-zoom)). While the grid is unlocked, dragging the waveform slides the
  audio under the grid, so you can line the beat lines up with the kicks by
  hand; one drag is one **Undo**. With the grid locked, dragging moves through
  the track as on the other layouts.
- On the left, deck 1's side of the [grid editor](music.md#grid-editor)
  (Type, Reset, BPM, Downbeat, Anchor and Grid), as in 2-Deck. Point at a
  button to see what it does and its key.
- **CUE** and play, beat jumps of 4, 8, 16 and 32 beats either way, a loop,
  and a thin level fader for the deck at the right.
- On the right, pads under four tabs:
  - **Hot Cues**: eight pads with their names and times. Click an empty pad
    to set it, a set one to jump there, Shift-click to clear it.
  - **Loops**: the track's saved loops in track order. Set a loop (IN and
    OUT, or the loop button), then click an empty pad to save it; click a
    saved one to jump there and play it looping, Shift-click to remove it.
  - **Automix**: **Mix in** and **Mix out**, where Automix brings the track
    in and starts mixing out of it. Click one to set it at the play line,
    click it again to jump there, Shift-click to clear it.
  - **Memory**: memory cues, markers on no pad that Pioneer players step
    through (they go to the stick with [USB export](usb-export.md)). Click an
    empty pad (or press **C**) to add one at the play line, a set one to
    jump there, Shift-click to remove it.
- **Enter** (Play Ready, next) locks the grid, moves the track to Play
  Ready and loads the next track In Prep.
  Cmd-Z (Ctrl-Z) takes the stage change back.

Keys in Prep: **Enter** Play Ready, next; **Tab** and **Shift-Tab** next and
previous phrase (8 bars at a time when the track has no phrases); **←** and
**→** four bars; **1**–**8** set or jump to a hot cue
(Shift clears it); **B** Bar 1 here; **T** tap; **M** the click; **[** and **]**
move the grid; **−** and **=** tempo; **L** lock; **C** or **Shift+T** memory cue. As in
rekordbox, **Z** plays and pauses, **A** is CUE, and **D**, **F** and **G** set the
loop in and out and turn it on and off. Space plays and pauses, as everywhere.

## Crossfader assignment

In 4-Deck, each deck says which side of the crossfader it is on: **Left**,
**Right** or **Thru** (not affected by the crossfader). Decks 1 and 3 start on
the left, 2 and 4 on the right. Click the pill to step to the next side.

## Decks 3 and 4 on a two-deck controller

A controller with two decks, such as the DDJ-FLX4, plays decks 1 and 2. In
4-Deck, click deck 3's number to move the controller's left side to deck 3,
and deck 1's number to move it back; deck 4's and deck 2's numbers do the same
for the right side. The number of each deck the controller plays is lit.
Switching to another layout moves both sides back to decks 1 and 2.

Automix always mixes between decks 1 and 2.
